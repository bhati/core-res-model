Domain Policy

(Design-Level Specification — Horizontal)

⸻

1. What Is Domain Policy

Domain Policy governs how Domain Expertise operates for known priors and foreseeable dangers. It is the equivalent of hospital guidelines for an in-house expert — not rules that block the expert, but governance that shapes how every expertise component functions when specific conditions are present.

Policy does not solve. Expertise solves. Policy ensures expertise solves safely.

⸻

2. Structure

Every domain policy reference has three layers:

2.1 Base Policies

Always active, regardless of user context. The safety floor.

Base policies govern every expertise component at a minimum standard:
	•	Knowledge — the system must have working domain knowledge before acting.
	•	Assessment — never recommend blindly; use whatever context is available.
	•	Recommendation — floor constraints (minimum safe thresholds, no harmful defaults).
	•	Communication — no guilt, no shame, no fear as motivators.
	•	Empathy — acknowledge the personal and emotional dimension of the domain.
	•	Evidence — present uncertain science honestly.
	•	Boundaries — scope limits (no diagnosis, no medical prescription, no therapy).
	•	Adaptation — absorb burden during difficult times.

Base policies are always on. They require no activation trigger.

2.2 Prior-Specific Policies

Activated when a known prior is present. Each prior governs the expertise components it materially affects.

A "known prior" is any declared or observed condition that creates foreseeable danger:
	•	Medical danger — conditions that interact with the domain (diabetes, pregnancy, kidney disease).
	•	Emotional danger — vulnerabilities that the domain could worsen (eating disorder history, body image sensitivity, past trauma).
	•	Physical danger — direct harm risks (allergens, drug-food interactions).
	•	Cultural/social danger — disrespecting food customs, religious rules, cultural norms.

For each known prior, policy defines how relevant expertise components are governed:

	•	Knowledge — what additional knowledge must be activated?
	•	Assessment — what must be assessed differently?
	•	Recommendation — what must be recommended differently?
	•	Communication — what language and framing is required?
	•	Empathy — what emotional considerations are heightened?
	•	Evidence — what clinical or domain guidelines must be consulted?
	•	Boundaries — what scope limits are tightened?
	•	Adaptation — how does adaptation change?

Not every prior governs all 8 components. Only materially affected components are specified.

2.3 Circumstance Modulation

Circumstances are temporary states that layer on top of active policy. They soften expectations without relaxing safety.

	•	Circumstances suggest adjustments — they don't dictate.
	•	Safety policies never relax under any circumstance.
	•	The system absorbs adherence burden during difficult times.
	•	When circumstances end, normalize gradually.

⸻

3. How Policy Relates to Expertise

Policy does not define expertise. It governs expertise. The relationship:

	•	Domain Expertise defines HOW the expert thinks, assesses, recommends, communicates, empathizes, reasons, bounds itself, and adapts.
	•	Domain Policy defines the CONDITIONS under which each component operates differently.

Same expert, different governance. A nutritionist helping a healthy 25-year-old operates differently from a nutritionist helping a pregnant diabetic — not because the expert changed, but because the governance did.

⸻

4. Danger Classification

Every domain should classify its foreseeable dangers:

	•	Medical — conditions where domain actions could cause physical harm.
	•	Emotional — vulnerabilities where domain actions could cause psychological harm.
	•	Physical — direct harm risks (allergens, toxicity, injury).
	•	Cultural/Social — risks of disrespecting identity, customs, or community norms.

Each danger type maps to known priors. Each prior activates governance across the relevant expertise components.

⸻

5. Policy Principles

	•	Policy governs, expertise solves. Policy shapes the expert; it does not replace the expert.
	•	Base is always on. Prior-specific activates per condition.
	•	Policy is the static library. Which priors are active for a given user is determined by context.
	•	Allergens and absolute safety exclusions are the only true hard cutoffs. Everything else is governance — elevated care, not prohibition.
	•	Policy must be transparent. The system should be able to explain why it's behaving the way it is.

⸻

6. What Policy Is Not

	•	Policy is not expertise — policy governs, expertise solves.
	•	Policy is not product principles — Product Principles are universal. Domain Policy is domain-specific.
	•	Policy is not context — context is what's true. Policy governs what to do about it.
	•	Policy is not orchestration — orchestration is the system. Policy is the governance the system operates under.

⸻

7. One-Line Definition

Domain Policy is the governance layer that shapes how every expertise component operates for known priors and foreseeable dangers — ensuring safety, emotional care, and cultural respect without replacing expert judgment.
